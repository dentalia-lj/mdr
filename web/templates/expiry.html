{% call ui.intro("What has already lapsed and what is about to — three separate questions, because they need three different answers.") %} This board was one table under a single horizon filter, which selected everything expiring within N days and so quietly included everything already expired. A certificate that lapsed in May with 1.046 items on it is a different and worse problem than one lapsing next month, and one table cannot say so.

A Declaration of Conformity usually carries no expiry of its own (MDR Annex IV requires only an issue date) — where it cites a notified-body certificate, this board shows the certificate's own date instead, marked inherited. On top of that every declaration is listed five years after issue, marked review — Dentalia ruled on 2026-08-18 that both apply, so whichever comes first is the date shown. It is a review horizon rather than an expiry: Article 19(1) asks that a declaration be kept continuously updated and names no validity period. Class I devices have no certificate at all, so review is the only trigger they will ever have. The manufacturer is a column rather than a heading because the soonest lapse can belong to any of them, and the manufacturer is who gets the email. {% endcall %}
