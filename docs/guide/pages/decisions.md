# Decisions

**In one sentence:** who decided what, and when — every decision the registry
has ever recorded, on one screen.

**Status:** Live. Checked against the code on 2026-09-15.

**This page is read-only.** Nothing here can be edited or removed. That is the
point of it: a record you could change is not a record.

---

## What this is

Every time a document is published, filed, rejected, superseded, or a link
between a document and a product is confirmed or withdrawn, the system writes a
line saying what happened, who did it and when. This page is that list.

Decisions made by a person carry your login. Decisions the system made on its
own carry the name of the part that made them.

---

## Reading a row

| Column | Means |
|---|---|
| **When** | The moment it was recorded |
| **What happened** | In words: *Approved*, *Rejected*, *Published*, *Replaced by a newer document*, *Item link withdrawn*, and so on |
| **Document** | The document it happened to, if any. Click through to it |
| **Item no.** | The product it happened to, if any |
| **Decided by** | The person or the part of the system that made the call |
| **Why** | The reason a reviewer gave, in their own words — for example *Out of date: newer 2025 version exists*. A dash means none was recorded |
| **Technical details** | The stored name of the event, the piece of work that carried it out, and anything else the decision recorded. The job link may lead nowhere for older rows, which is expected — see below |

Filter by what happened, or search for a person, a product number or a document
number. The filter list shows the same words as the rows.

---

## Why a job link can lead nowhere

Each row carries its own copy of what happened, so it stays readable even after
the piece of work behind it has been cleaned away. The job link under
**Technical details** is a pointer, not the record. When it leads nowhere,
nothing has been lost: the row still says what was decided, by whom, and when.

---

## When you would use it

- An auditor asks who approved a particular declaration, and on what date.
- A document is published and you want to know whether a person accepted it or
  the system did.
- A document was rejected and you want to know why. The reason the reviewer
  chose on [Review](review.md) is on its row.
- Something looks wrong and you want the history before you change it.
